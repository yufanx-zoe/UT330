Building a parks department website

September 11, 2026 · Week 2

I finished a four-page website for Riverton Parks & Recreation. The assignment started as a partially built department site, and my job was to turn the content into a complete, usable public-service website.

What I built

I completed the homepage and three interior pages: Parks, Reservations, and Rules & Contact. The homepage became the starting point for common tasks, like finding a park, reserving a shelter, checking trail rules, and reporting a maintenance problem.

The interior pages organize the same service system in more detail. The Parks page compares Riverfront Park, Maple Commons, and Northside Sports Complex. The Reservations page explains fees and permits. The Rules & Contact page gathers dog rules, trail rules, events, accessibility, and contact information.

Design choices

I kept the retro municipal style from the reference site: window panels, simple navigation, badges, notices, tables, and a visitor counter. I tried to make the site feel like an old city webpage while still making the information easy to scan.

What mattered

This project was less about decorating a page and more about information architecture. A parks department website has to answer practical questions: Where can I go? What can I do there? How much does it cost? Who do I call if something is broken?

What I learned

Static HTML can still do a lot when the structure is clear. Headings, tables, links, breadcrumbs, and current-page navigation all make the site easier to use. The most important design move was putting the right information in the right place.
